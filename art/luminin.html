the luminin

A visual instrument: it turns a performer's motion into light.

A simulation of the first Luminin. Move the guitar, and the controller on its headstock drives the sketch, which lights the two screens.

Just as a musical instrument transmutes the movements of a skilled musician into intricate arrangements of sonic vibrations, the Luminin, a “visual instrument,” converts the physical motions of its performer into a dazzling light show.

And it isn't tied to its own screens: from a club stage to a gallery, it can play whatever lights and video a venue has, as it did at Baby's All Right.

how it works

The controller sits on the guitar's headstock. Inside it, a motion sensor (an accelerometer and gyroscope) feels how the guitar tilts, swings and jolts, twenty times a second, and its own small grid of lights shows what it feels.

It sends those readings through the air to a small receiver wired to a Raspberry Pi. The Pi runs a Processing sketch, which turns them into a series of animations, live.

The animations play across two hand-built 26 × 52 inch LED screens: 512 lights each, behind paper that softens them into fields of colour.

Tilting the guitar shifts the sketch's colours.

the concept

  • Played live. The light comes from a performer's movement in the moment, so it keeps the risk and life of live playing.
  • A score, not eye candy. A light show is to a song what a film score is to a film: it serves the music.
  • Screens as canvas. Sprites are brushes leaving temporary trails of paint; how long trails last, their colour and motion set the mood.

By tying movement to visual output, and bestowing a performer with power to modify and manipulate their visual environment with a flick of the wrist, the Luminin opens the door for a new artistic language: a syntax for the choreography of light and image.

Modular in design and built with cheap, easy to obtain materials, the Luminin is a proof of concept for a DIY platform which would provide musicians and performers with the tools to design and innovate the visual aspects of their performance while on a budget.

The first Luminin was my MFA thesis at Pratt, in 2021. The thesis goes far deeper into the technical details and philosophical motivations behind the project: download the pdf.
