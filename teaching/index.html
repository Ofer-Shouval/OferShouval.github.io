teaching

I made a thing, and now I spend a lot of my time teaching other people to make things.

I've taught at the college level since 2021 (interaction design and creative coding at NYU, physical computing at Parsons, audio production at Pratt), and it's become one of the most satisfying parts of my practice, probably because it's a little like playing a show. The subjects are different, but the approach is the same.

first, a crash course

A run of quick labs and small homework projects that build on each other and put students in direct contact with the tools before they've had time to be intimidated by them.

then: what do you want to make?

That question goes hand in hand with learning to research: how to face a page of techno-gobbledygook without panicking, and how to find the person who already solved your problem. There are many things wrong with the internet, but it's an incredibly powerful tool for learning how to make things, and I want my students to know that side of it too.

then I step back

For the final project I become more of a facilitator. I keep giving demos as topics come up, but most of the time goes to one-on-one conversations about each person's project. That's where the spectacular stuff happens, and where collaborations start that outlast the class. One of my former physical computing students plays bass in my band.

the catch

An open question like that can be a lot, and some students get lost in it. With undergrads I give more structured assignments before letting them loose. Even for the ones who struggle, I think it's a freeing process. Some people do their best work inside an external structure, and that's a good thing to know about yourself.

courses

NYU · 2025–present

Interaction Design Studio

A seminar on interaction design as both a creative and a design practice. Students from all kinds of backgrounds work in groups, picking up code, hardware and digital-media tools, and looking hard at how technology shapes the way people relate to each other. Co-taught with Camilla Morales.

NYU · 2021–present

Creative Coding

An introduction to programming for students who've never written a line of code. They learn to think about software as a creative medium, and by the end they can read and write code for interactive things: websites, installations, games and more.

Pratt Digital Arts · 2026–present

Audio for Digital Media

Sound as a storytelling tool for animation. Students go out with field recorders, perform foley, record voiceover and learn to edit and mix in Pro Tools, then finish the semester by designing all the sound for their own 2D animated film.

Parsons MFA Design & Technology · 2021–2025

Physical Computing

Screens, trackpads and keyboards use a tiny fraction of the senses we evolved to deal with the physical world. In this class we "play around" with microcontrollers, sensors, motors and LEDs, from circuits and Arduino to soldering, and each student ends the semester with a working interactive object: a kinetic sculpture, an instrument, a wearable.

Many of my class demos grew into finished pieces — they live in the wormhole.
